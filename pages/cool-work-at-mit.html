Cool Work at MIT

I got to do a lot of cool work during my undergrad and masters.

I did research at CSAIL (CS + AI Lab) using clang to rewrite C code as a part of an automated process of turning C into Rust for my thesis.

I also took some really great classes. They solidified my understanding of what happens underneath the software abstraction and taught me how to use that knowledge effectively for maximum performance:

Top 3

  • 6.S894 Accelerated Computing
  • 6.1060 Software Performance Engineering
  • 6.1100 Computer Language Engineering

Honorable Mentions

  • 6.1810 Operating Systems Engineering
  • 6.1920 Constructive Computer Architecture
  • 6.5840 Distributed Systems
  • 6.5930 Hardware Architecture for Deep Learning

6.1810 showed me how page tables, syscalls like fork() and mmap(), traps, and the filesystem are implemented under the hood. It did this by letting students get hands-on experience by directly modifying the source of a simple, unix-like OS.

6.1920 taught me CPU microarchitecture from the ground up including superscalar, SMT, cache coherence, pipelining, store buffers etc.

6.5840 discussed many papers on various distributed systems like MapReduce (of course), GFS, Spanner, Zookeeper, etc and gave me the chance to fully implement Raft in Go

6.5930 helped me connect my GPU knowledge with how actual ML/AI workloads would use them. My final project investigated how to address bottlenecks in flash attention.
