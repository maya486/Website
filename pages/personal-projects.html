Personal Projects

My goal with my personal projects is usually some combination of 1) do something cool and 2) get actual experience coding in the area of CS I am interested in (reading books can only do so much). CS is really a learn-by-doing field. The whole point of software construction is building something. Especially something like performance engineering where you have to be a bit creative with your solutions, getting hands-on experience has been helpful and fun in my experience.

This past summer I had a lot of free time between finishing school and waiting for my job to start, so I decided to put my efforts into some performance engineering problems I had heard about: Anthropic Performance Take-Home and Highload Challenges. I picked these in particular because they were interesting, didn’t require owning the hardware yourself (no AVX512 on my Macbook 💀), and had robust ways of measuring performance.

In the future I plan on doing something related to networking/systems. I would also like to do some more GPU work, but renting those out, especially the bare metal environments needed for reliable performance measurements, are a bit pricier compared to a linux host. Once I’ve exhausted my systems goals and I spend the time to make a solid plan of what type of problem I’d like to do on a GPU, I’ll relook into that.

Note on AI: AI is VERY good at programming, which is great if you want something else to do your programming. The point of these personal projects, though, is 1) love of the game and 2) learning. I don’t want something else doing the hard part of idea generation and understanding the problem down to the fine implementation details which is why I try to limit my AI usage in these personal projects to just debugging and I’d recommend that to other people too. The one notable exception being is the creation of this website because I am not a UI person.
